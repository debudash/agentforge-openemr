Is the co-pilot safe, honest and fast enough today?

A rolled-up view of the agent's own /metrics endpoint, written for the people who own the service rather than the people who debug it. Every figure here is a counter the running service keeps; none of it is estimated.

Snapshot, not live. Taken from the development instance on 2026-09-20. Volumes are small — 3 sessions, 3 briefs, 3 answers — so the percentiles below are shown for shape, not for judgement. Percentiles need roughly 20 samples before they mean anything.

Access denied is deliberately not red. A refusal is the boundary holding, not a failure, and a dashboard that paints it as an error teaches the opposite. The state to watch is unable: it means the machinery broke or nothing survived verification.

Was it fast enough?

Answers are measured against the PRD's interactive targets; briefs against the 90-second prefetch budget, because a brief is generated before the clinician opens the patient and is reused until that chart changes. The p95 miss on answers is the known tradeoff: a verified answer costs at least two model calls, and nothing is streamed before it is checked.

What this view cannot tell you yet

  • Verified claim rate is not derivable. The service counts claims_removed but not claims kept, so precision — the headline safety number — cannot be computed from /metrics. One counter would fix it.
  • Counters reset on restart. They live in process memory alongside everything else, so this is "since the service last started", not a period.
  • No per-clinician or per-ward breakdown. Deliberate: the metrics endpoint carries no identifiers, so it cannot become a second place PHI lives.
  • Time saved is still unmeasured. The workflow promise has no baseline, and no counter here will produce one — it needs a clinician with a stopwatch.
