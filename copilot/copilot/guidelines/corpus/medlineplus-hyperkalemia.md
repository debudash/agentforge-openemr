## Definition

A high potassium level is a problem in which the amount of potassium in the blood is higher than normal. The medical name of this condition is hyperkalemia.

## Causes

If your kidneys are not working well, they may not be able to remove the proper amount of potassium.

Certain blood pressure lowering medicines, most often angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs), and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (such as spironolactone).

Other causes named: Addison disease, extensive burns, muscle damage, hemolytic anemia, severe gastrointestinal bleeding, excess potassium intake, and tumors.

## Symptoms

There are often no symptoms with a high level of potassium. When present, symptoms include chest pain, difficulty breathing, nausea or vomiting, palpitations, and a slow, weak, or irregular pulse, or sudden collapse.

## Exams and tests

Testing includes a basic metabolic panel, an electrocardiogram (ECG), and a serum potassium level. The provider performs kidney function blood tests for at-risk patients taking potassium supplements, those with chronic kidney disease, or individuals on heart or blood pressure medications.
